# Warm Visitor DM — the hybrid for identified website visitors

This is the framework the generator writes against for identified visitors. It blends the
two DM types rather than picking one, because an identified visitor sits between them.

## Why a blend

A pure **Conversion DM** assumes the sub-audience was already built through prior
conversation, so it opens straight on the offer. Used cold on a website visitor it reads
generic — nothing in it proves you know anything about this specific person, and it lands
like every other cold email.

A pure **Audience-Building DM** assumes the prospect hasn't self-identified yet, so it
spends its energy surfacing pain to find out. A site visitor already answered that.

What a site visitor gives you that neither assumes: **they came to you**, and enrichment
knows their actual stack. So take the Audience-Building DM's sharpest weapon — a specific,
personalized observation that names their pain in their own terms — and fuse it onto the
Conversion DM's offer and CTA.

## The structure

1. **Warm open — acknowledge the visit.** One short line. "Thanks for stopping by the site."
   Human and direct. This is not a cold email and shouldn't pretend to be.

2. **The personalized pain hook** (Audience-Building DM step 1–4, compressed to one or two
   sentences). Name their actual tools and state the pain as a specific consequence:
   *"You're running HubSpot, Stripe and Mailchimp — that's three different answers to
   'how did last month go?'"* This is the line the whole email lives or dies on. It must be
   something you could only write to this company.

3. **The solution, in one line** (Audience-Building DM's "there's a new way" compressed).
   No feature list, no process explanation.

4. **The CTA — offer the sample dashboard built on their stack.** This is the approved ask.
   It is low friction, it's a gift rather than a meeting request, and it proves the claim
   instead of asserting it.

5. **A soft open door.** Invite them to say what actually brought them in, without asking
   the dead question "are you interested?". Something like *"or tell me what pulled you in
   and I'll point you at the right thing."*

Sign-off. No title, no footer, no postscript.

## Length

**45–85 words.** Shorter than a normal email on purpose — this should read like something a
person typed, not something a company sent. If a sentence isn't doing work, cut it.

## The 4 universal pains, applied here

Lead with *make more money* (revenue the reports can't attribute) or *live a better life*
(hours lost rebuilding the same report). Say it as a consequence they'd recognize, never as
an abstraction. Numbers beat adjectives.

## What kills these

- Naming the tools but not the consequence. "You use HubSpot and Stripe" is not a pain.
- Explaining the service. They visited the site; they know what we do.
- Any sentence that could be sent to a different company unchanged.
- Claiming to have watched their behaviour. Acknowledging the visit is warm; describing
  which pages they viewed or how long they stayed is not.
- Asking "is this something you'd be interested in?" — dead question, dead reply rate.
