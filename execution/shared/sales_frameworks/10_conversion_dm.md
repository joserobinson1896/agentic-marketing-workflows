# Conversion DM Framework

Source: client's own playbook, Step 5. **This is the framework the generator writes against.**

## Purpose

Turn a warm audience — people already in pain — into buyers and clients. Move from attention to
transaction. You are not identifying pain at this stage; that's already established. You're speaking
to someone who knows the pain, understands the problem, and just needs the next step.

## The principle of the offer

Every Conversion DM has one central job: **make a specific, relevant offer, fast.**

You're not explaining your system or process in detail anymore. You're making an invitation to act.
Overcomplicating the message kills momentum. Keep it conversational, short and direct.

The speed at which you make offers determines how quickly you get results. Build conversion DMs to be
offer-driven.

## The framework

1. **Choose a specific promise**
2. **Add ethical urgency and scarcity**
3. **Lead with price** (if selling by chat)
4. **End with a call to action**

## Examples (match this length and register)

> NAME,
>
> I'm opening the doors for 10 people this week to work with me and take their FB profile to an extra
> $3k cash-profits a week on autopilot in under 8 weeks.
>
> I've made it easy to get started for just $100. Can I share more?

> Hi NAME, I'm doing something new with 20 founders next week, and you might be in the market for this…
>
> We'll produce and install the 3 Digital Assets of my Income Guarantee™, so you generate 3 sales leads
> daily and $3k in new MRR each month, like clockwork, even if you're on a long vacation.
>
> Made it accessible for just $300 and added a "no questions asked" money-back guarantee.
> Shall I send you the deets?

> Hi NAME,
>
> We just developed a new strategy that helps agencies book 3–5 qualified calls weekly using DMs,
> without ads or content.
>
> I'm walking 10 founders through it this week.
>
> Do you have 45 minutes today or tomorrow to chat about it?

## Register notes drawn from those examples

- 3–5 short sentences. Line breaks between beats. No preamble, no pleasantries beyond the name.
- Plain sentence case. No corporate voice, no em-dash-heavy prose, no bullet lists.
- Specific numbers, never vague claims ("3–5 qualified calls weekly", "under 8 weeks", "$3k").
- Scarcity is a real constraint on capacity ("10 people this week", "20 founders next week"), not a
  fake deadline.
- Ends on a low-friction question that asks permission, not commitment: "Can I share more?",
  "Shall I send you the deets?", "Do you have 45 minutes today or tomorrow?"

## Follow-ups and reoffers

- Follow up 2–3 times within a conversion DM cycle.
- Every 7–14 days, send a **reoffer**: a new Conversion DM, not a check-in. Same offer, different
  angle — speed, certainty, support. Turning the same diamond to catch a different kind of buyer.
- 60% of customers say no four times before saying yes. One DM and disappearing wastes the audience.
- Larger enterprises may need longer cycles.

## Applying this to Unified Dashboards

- **Channel: email.** The framework is written for DMs, but the register carries over unchanged —
  short, offer-driven, conversational, no corporate voice. The only additions email requires are a
  subject line and a one-line sign-off. Do **not** let it drift into a formal email: no "I hope this
  finds you well", no paragraphs, no bulleted value props, no long signature block. If the body would
  read fine as a DM, it's right.
- **Pain**: primarily *live a better life* (hours lost rebuilding the same report) and *make more
  money* (revenue the current reports can't attribute).
- **The promise** is built from the lead's own detected stack — name their actual tools.
- **Scarcity** is genuine delivery capacity, since this is a done-for-you service.
- **CTA** is a walkthrough or a sample dashboard, never "start a free trial" — there is no self-serve
  product to start.
